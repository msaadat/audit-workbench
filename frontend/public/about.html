The auditor of the future

AI is already in your audit file.

  • Drafting memos. Summarising policies. Writing up findings.
  • Not by policy — because it works, and the deadline is real.
  • The question is no longer whether auditors use AI. It is whether the file behind the report still holds up when they do.

A model will not replace the auditor. But an auditor without proper tools will be replaced by one who has them — and if those tools are a browser tab, assurance quality falls while the documents get better-looking every year.

Machine does the assemblyReconciliation, extraction, sampling — most of an audit was never auditing

↓ Start with the obvious objection

The obvious objection

“So why not just upload the folder and ask ChatGPT?”

You can. Ten minutes gets you a planning memorandum, a risk matrix and a draft report that read well enough to circulate.

⚠Hallucination is the risk everyone names — and the least interesting one. It is the failure you are already watching for. The dangerous failures look right.

It returns the median answer

  • Trained on how audits are usually described
  • Lists the risks any audit of this type would list
  • An averaging machine — it cannot be surprised
  • It has never been in the warehouse, and does not know two contract managers left in March

It has no judgment to apply

  • No view on materiality, sufficiency of evidence, sampling adequacy or scope limitation
  • Weights findings by prominence in the text, not by risk
  • Regenerate, and the auditor's corrections are gone
  • Nobody can be held to account for the output

Human in the loop, by construction

Same model. Different workplace.

No proprietary audit intelligence — the same class of model you would use in a browser. Everything that makes the difference is built around it.

A full transaction population cannot go in a prompt — so a chatbot samples, approximates, or writes code you never see.

Populations are computed locally, in full. The model receives schemas, statistics and bounded previews only.

A chat thread holds a paragraph. Six months later, someone asks how you know.

Immutable evidence anchors carry the source hash, the page and the exact text used.

Ask twice, get two answers — with no way to tell a data change from model drift.

Results carry hashes and table fingerprints. Tests are durable and re-runnable.

It will always give you an answer — a confident final report on work that was never done.

Open material testing marks the report preliminary, through a deterministic check no model output can clear.

Regenerate, and the auditor's edits are silently overwritten.

Ask mode pauses at editable approvals; regeneration reconciles side by side. Runs pause, resume and survive restarts.

One instruction — “prepare a full audit working draft” — planned and executed a 60-task run: SOP and minutes read, scope and risk matrix drafted, tests prepared, working papers and report draft generated.

A chatbot is a brilliant intern who has read everything, has no memory, never shows their working, and will never tell you they didn't finish. This is the firm around that intern — a desk, a filing system, a supervisor's sign-off, and a rule that nothing goes out until the work is done.

The most valuable thing this product does is refuse to finish.

Where accountability lives

Somebody signs.

  • It is that AI would do it invisibly — output accepted, file never built.
  • Quality falls while the documents look better than ever.
  • The answer: make the fast path and the defensible path the same path.
Auditor approval

Proposed changes pause for review before they are applied.

Evidence retained

Tests, observations and findings keep their supporting links.

Completion gates

Open material work prevents an unsupported final conclusion.

Controlled environment

Engagement data stays in the auditor's workspace. Full populations never leave the machine.

Twenty-five items out of a population of tens of thousands was never a methodological choice — it was a labour constraint. Removing it does not mean a smaller team. It means the same weeks spent differently: less reconciliation, more time on intent, on scope, and on the conversation management would rather not have. The hours move up, not away.

Every finding keeps its links back to the test, the evidence and the risk it came from.

We didn't build an AI that audits. We built the place where an auditor can safely let one help.
