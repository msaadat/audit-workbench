The auditor of the future

AI is already in your audit file.

  • Drafting memos. Summarising policies. Writing up findings.
  • Not by policy — because it works, and the deadline is real.
  • The question is no longer whether auditors use AI. It is whether the file behind the report still holds up when they do.

A model will not replace the auditor. But an auditor without proper tools will be replaced by one who has them — and if those tools are a browser tab, assurance quality falls while the documents get better-looking every year.

The second objection

“Isn't it just a workflow anyone could rebuild?”

The workflow, yes. It's audit methodology, and a good team with AI coding tools could demo it in weeks. Making it trustworthy takes far longer. That's what we own.

01 · RulesWhere AI work gets rejected

31 checkpoints, each verified by software. Drafts that cite missing data, skip a required section or check half the documents are thrown out. Many rules came from real failures, and 2,500+ automated tests hold them in place.

02 · ProofScored against answer keys

Practice engagements with exceptions planted in the data, in the documents, and in the gap between them. We count what it finds. On procurement, analytics alone find 18 of 28, before any AI.

03 · IndependenceRules that outlive the model

The controls sit in the software, so a better model makes the product better, not obsolete. The methodology belongs to the firm, not to an AI vendor.

Anyone can rebuild the workflow. The IP is knowing, and proving, where AI cuts corners in it.
